# Screen Score #1 — Codex rubric amendments

8 October 2026, US Central. Step 1 only. Read the brief and draft; no renders or previous screen reviews inspected, no scores assigned. Keep the six criteria and their equal weights, with these changes before Pete settles the rubric.

1. **Fix the unit of scoring and aggregation.** Score every manifest render separately. Then average available viewport scores within each logical screen/state, and average those logical screen/state scores for the game score. Publish per-viewport means and the worst findings beside it. Use unrounded values for aggregation; round only display values. This prevents screens with more rendered viewports receiving extra weight. Missing viewports explicitly excluded by the brief are not defects. Every manifest row must appear in the eventual results.
2. **Anchor the middle scores.** 1 = severe failure of the criterion; 2 = major friction; 3 = usable with a clear weakness; 4 = good with minor issues; 5 = meets the stated criterion. Support deductions with findings; uncertainty alone is not a deduction. C2 should say the primary action is easy to locate and appropriately prominent: informative content can reasonably be the dominant element on a report.
3. **Limit claims to the evidence.** In C1, replace “in under 3 seconds” with “purpose and next action are apparent from the render”; a timed first-user claim needs a usability measurement. In C3, score readability at the documented canvas/presentation scale. Do not claim physical-device legibility, touch feel or controller usability from a still. Tight English text can prompt a translation-fit check; unseen translation failures cannot lower the observed score.
4. **Make C4 judge useful fit, not compulsory stretching.** A 5 respects safe areas and places readable, reachable content appropriately. Purposeful margins can score 5; unexplained bands warrant a deduction when they create a demonstrable layout weakness or appear broken. Record width, region and lost opportunity before suggesting expansion. Preserve the locked list aspect ratio and pixel-art rules. The handoff's wide-screen concern is a hypothesis to inspect after agreement, not a preassigned penalty.
5. **Separate observation, impact and validation.** A finding records [seen] evidence plus [judgement] impact where necessary; add [needs validation] for behavioral claims. An empty band can be seen; whether it confuses a player is a judgment. Assign S1 when essential information is wrong/unreadable or an essential action is blocked, S2 for meaningful friction, S3 for polish. Deduplicate a cross-viewport defect while citing each affected render. List S1/S2 counts beside the mean so severe failures cannot disappear in an average.
6. **Prioritize severity before effort.** Rank S1 fixes first, then S2, then S3; use impact/effort within those groups. S/M/L are rough implementation estimates, not promised delivery times. Label C6 and taste-based consistency judgments clearly and tie them to the brief's theme and art constraints.

The eventual score table should add viewport and screen/state identifiers. The existing finding IDs, evidence paths, concrete suggestions and limitations section otherwise work. No `codex-score.md` until Pete has settled the rubric and authorized Step 2, as required by this folder's README.
